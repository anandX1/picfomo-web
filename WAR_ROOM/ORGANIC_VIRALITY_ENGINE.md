# Organic Virality Engine

## 1. The Psychology of Vanity Sharing
People do not share AI tools because they love the software; they share them because they love how the software makes *them* look. Sharing an AI portrait is a socially acceptable way to post a highly flattering photo of oneself without seeming narcissistic ("Look what this crazy AI did!").

## 2. The "Delta" Fascination (Before/After)
Virality in visual AI isn't driven by the final image; it is driven by the *Delta*—the distance between the input and the output.
* A great headshot is boring.
* A messy bedhead selfie turning into a 4K Vogue editorial in 20 seconds is a magic trick.
* **The Engine:** PicFomo must auto-generate a shareable "Before/After" slider GIF or side-by-side composite for every user to post on X/TikTok.

## 3. OpenGraph Psychology
When a user shares their `/post/[id]` URL on LinkedIn or Twitter:
* **Current Meta:** "PicFomo - AI Portrait."
* **Viral Meta:** The OpenGraph image must dynamically render the user's stunning portrait with an overlay: *"Generated in 20 Seconds on PicFomo."*
* **The Trigger:** Peers see the image, feel status anxiety, read "20 Seconds," and immediately click the link to upgrade their own profile. The OG tag acts as a behavioral hook.

## 4. Social Proof Loops
* **The Network Effect:** On LinkedIn, updating a profile picture notifies a user's entire network. If the picture is noticeably exceptional, peers will ask in the comments, "Who was the photographer?" 
* **The Feature:** Prompt the user immediately after generation: *"Update LinkedIn now? Click here to copy this caption: 'Just updated my headshot in 20 seconds without leaving my desk using @PicFomo.'"*

## 5. Emotional Aesthetics
Generic corporate headshots do not go viral. Extreme aesthetics do.
* **The Strategy:** While the "Corporate" style pays the bills, the "Cyberpunk," "90s Yearbook," and "Old Money" styles drive the viral top-of-funnel traffic. 
* Feature these hyper-stylized generations on the `/explore` feed to maximize shareability on TikTok and Pinterest.
